Ethics Guidelines and Grievance Procedures

Mountain Colors Zen · Draft 10.23.2026

Need for a Code of Ethics

In order for Buddhist practice to proceed in an environment that is respectful, safe, and supportive for all members of the Mountain Colors Zen (MCZ) Sangha, a Code of Ethics grounded in the Buddhist Precepts must be established. The MCZ Code of Ethics, under the guidance of the MCZ Board of Directors, specifies the nature of ethical behavior within the Sangha, clarifies the expectation for ethical behavior for all Sangha members and participants in MCZ sponsored activities, and protocols for handling grievances should they arise.

The Buddhist Precepts

The Precepts form the core of Buddhist Practice. They act as guideposts in our effort to actualize good for all beings. Within the MCZ Sangha, and during MCZ sponsored activities, it is expected that practitioners will faithfully endeavor to follow the precepts.

The Three Treasures

  • I take refuge in the Buddha — To trust and rely upon enlightened wisdom
  • I take refuge in the Dharma — To trust and rely upon enlightened teachings and practices
  • I take refuge in the Sangha — To trust and rely upon the community of practitioners.

The Three Pure Precepts

  • Not creating evil — All harmful actions arise from the human mind. Cease from all harming. Care about people, all living things, and the Great Earth and do not cause harm.
  • Practicing good — All beneficial actions arise from the human mind. Bring positive, life-affirming actions into the world in any way and at any time.
  • Actualizing good for others — Offer kind words and loving actions towards others through simple actions, gifts and by cultivating trust.

The Ten Grave Precepts

  1. Affirm life; Do not kill — Affirm life by seeing all things as sacred, as worthy of respect and do not carelessly disrespect, desecrate, or destroy life.
  2. Be giving; Do not steal — Cultivate generosity and non-clinging. Respect appropriate boundaries and do not take what is not yours.
  3. Honor the body; Do not misuse sexuality — See all people as whole, miraculous, complex beings worthy of respect and possessing dignity. Guard your own sexual impulses, through your thoughts, words and actions, and do not impose them on others.
  4. Manifest truth; Do not lie — In the practice of realizing what is true and real, be honest in body, speech and mind. Strengthen the bonds of trust with others by being truthful and straightforward.
  5. Proceed clearly; Do not cloud the mind — Honor your bright, clear mind of awareness and do not cloud it with intoxicants in an effort to avoid or deny what needs to be practiced.
  6. See the perfection; Do not speak of others’ errors and faults — See each person as a marvelous and beautiful universe of evolution, humanity, and karmic streams. Speak of others’ faults only when this would help them, rather than to cause them pain.
  7. Realize self and other as one; Do not elevate the self and blame others — Within our differences, we all share universal, human qualities. Practice humility and do not attempt to feel better by denigrating others.
  8. Give generously; Do not be withholding — You always have something — time, attention, a word, assistance — to offer someone. Be generous in giving what you have to offer that might help another.
  9. Actualize harmony; Do not be angry — Anger is a hot, fast-moving, and often self-righteous force. Take responsibility for your own anger, irritation, impatience, etc., and don’t direct your anger outwards onto others.
  10. Experience the intimacy of things; Do not defile the three treasures — Appreciate the gift of Dharma practice and your ability to train within Buddha, Dharma and Sangha. If you are troubled or upset, work with this skillfully and don’t direct it towards the Three Treasures.

Sexual Harassment and Sexual Misconduct

MCZ explicitly prohibits sexual harassment, sexual misconduct, and retaliation for reporting such behavior. According to New York State law, sexual harassment includes unwanted verbal or physical behavior of a sexual nature that creates a hostile, threatening, or offensive environment or interferes with an individual’s ability to participate in Sangha life with trust and a sense of personal safety. Sexual harassment also includes jokes, or persistent or unwelcome comments or remarks about an individual’s gender, gender identity, gender expression, clothing, or perceived sexual orientation. Harassment based on actual or perceived sex, sexual orientation, gender identity, or gender expression — including discrimination against transgender and non-binary individuals — is explicitly prohibited.

Sexual misconduct is a broader ethical category that includes any sexual behavior or boundary violation that breaches the trust, safety, or integrity of relationships within the Sangha. It includes:

  1. Sexual harassment (as defined above).
  2. Abuse of spiritual, hierarchical, or institutional power for sexual purposes.
  3. Sexual or romantic relationships where true consent is compromised by authority, secrecy, or coercion.
  4. Repeated inappropriate sexualized conduct, even if not legally defined as harassment.

Role of the MCZ Board of Directors

The MCZ Board of Directors is responsible for administering and upholding the ethical standards as outlined in this policy. It is also responsible for responding to official complaints which includes investigating grievances and determining next steps. The Board will also determine what information can be shared with the Sangha. The Board is responsible for:

  • Receiving complaints
  • When possible, facilitating an informal process for conflict resolution
  • Conducting impartial investigations
  • Determining factual findings based on a preponderance of the evidence
  • Responsible for enforcement or implementation of any recommendation.

Grievance Process

Informal Process

When safe and appropriate, Sangha members are encouraged to first seek resolution to interpersonal conflicts through direct communication. This is not appropriate in cases of sexual misconduct, or other serious harm. Direct resolution might include:

  • A one-on-one conversation with the other party.
  • A dialogue facilitated by a member of the MCZ Board. If desired by both parties, mediation or facilitated dialogue may be an option if determined to be appropriate by the Board. Typically, mediation will involve a Board Member with whom both parties are comfortable.
  • The process is intended to foster clarity, repair, and mutual understanding.

Formal Complaint

If informal resolution is not possible, appropriate, or successful, Sangha members may submit a written complaint to the Board. Confidentiality will be protected to the greatest extent possible, balancing privacy with the need for a fair investigation. The submission should include:

  • A description of the concern and key events.
  • Names of the individuals involved.
  • Any actions taken to date.

Review Procedure

The Board will acknowledge receipt of formal written complaints within 72 hours.

The Board will strive to make an initial review of the complaint within 15 days of receipt and will update the complainant on the timeline if this is not possible. The review may include, but is not limited to: gathering evidence, conducting interviews with parties involved and witnesses, and reviewing relevant documentation. Reviews are conducted impartially, and recommendations will be based on a “preponderance of the evidence” standard (i.e., determining whether it is more likely than not that the misconduct occurred).

Confidentiality will be protected to the greatest extent possible, balancing privacy with the need for a fair investigation.

Possible Outcomes and Recommendations

Upon completion of the review, the Board will prepare a written report detailing the investigative findings and recommended responses. The written report will include recommendations on behalf of the complainant and on behalf of the respondent. Responses may include:

  • Mediation or facilitated dialogue*
  • Formal warning
  • Apology, amends, or restorative practices that may include the larger Sangha or Sangha cohorts
  • Required training (e.g., power dynamics, harm involving race or gender roles, or trauma)
  • Recommended psychotherapy or professional counseling
  • Role changes within the Sangha
  • Temporary suspension from community roles
  • Referral to the Board for fiduciary, legal, or financial concerns
  • Structured reintegration into Sangha activities
  • A shared agreement (e.g. memorandum of understanding) may be created to support accountability and reconciliation
  • Separation so that the complainant can practice without contact with the respondent
  • Permanent exclusion in cases of severe or repeated harm
  • Referral to legal authorities in cases involving potential criminal conduct

*Mediation is not utilized in cases of sexual misconduct to maintain clear accountability and safety. All allegations of sexual misconduct proceed directly to a formal investigation.

Conclusion

MCZ is committed to building a practice environment that is safe, respectful, and accountable. This document serves to support this goal by specifying the nature of ethical conduct and procedures that will be followed when the expectation for ethical behavior is breached. We believe that when members of the MCZ Sangha dedicate themselves to following the ethical precepts wholeheartedly, a practice community rooted in compassion, justice, and ethical conduct will be built.
